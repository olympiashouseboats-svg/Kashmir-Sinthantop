Overview & Key Facts

Sinthan Top is a high-altitude mountain pass in the Anantnag district of Jammu & Kashmir, India. It sits at approximately 12,000 feet (3,658–3,800 metres) above sea level and serves as a crossing point between the Kashmir Valley on its northern side and the Kishtwar district of Jammu division on its southern side. The pass is motorable and maintained by the Border Roads Organisation (BRO).

History & Historical Significance

Sinthan Top's history is woven into Kashmir Valley's connection with the Jammu hill regions. Before motorable roads existed, the pass served as a traditional migratory route for the Gujjar and Bakerwal nomadic communities — pastoralists who have moved livestock between the plains of Jammu and the summer pastures of Kashmir Valley for centuries.

The modern motorable road was constructed and is maintained by the Border Roads Organisation (BRO), opening Sinthan Top to tourism while maintaining strategic connectivity between the Kashmir Valley and Jammu divisions. In recent years it has been "discovered" by adventure tourists, photographers and honeymooners. Our agency, based in Daksum at the foot of the road, has been part of this emerging tourism from the beginning.

Geography & Geological Setting

Sinthan Top sits within the Lesser Himalayan range transitional zone, between the Pir Panjal sub-range and the Greater Himalayan crystalline rocks. To the north, terrain descends through the Bringi River valley into the Kashmir Valley; to the south, the descent into Kishtwar is steeper, entering the gorge country of the Chenab River tributaries. The alpine meadows (margs) around the summit are glacially-sculpted bowls, supporting rich high-altitude grassland and wildflower communities.

Altitude, Elevation & What to Expect at 12,000ft

At 12,000 feet, the air contains roughly 63% of sea-level oxygen. For most healthy adults arriving from the Kashmir Valley (already at ~1,600m), the ascent is within tolerable limits for a day trip, but worth preparing for. Common effects include mild headache, breathlessness on exertion, and mild fatigue — typically resolving within 20–30 minutes of rest.

⚠️ Altitude safety: Anyone with heart conditions, respiratory problems, or a history of altitude sickness should consult a doctor first. We recommend a 20-minute stop in Daksum (~2,400m) before continuing to the summit. If symptoms of AMS appear — severe headache, vomiting, confusion — descend immediately.

Route 2: From Jammu / Kishtwar Side

Approach via the Jammu-Kishtwar road (Jammu to Sinthan Top ~330 km, ~115 km from Kishtwar itself). Longer, more dramatic mountain conditions, less commonly used by tourists — popular with motorcyclists doing the Kashmir-Kishtwar circuit. Professional local drivers are essential.

Route 3: From Pahalgam

Pahalgam to Sinthan Top is approximately 55 km via Anantnag, roughly 1.5–2 hours, passing through Kokernag and Daksum.

Weather & Temperature

Even on a warm summer day in Srinagar (30°C), the Sinthan Top summit can be 15–20°C cooler. Sudden weather changes are common — a clear morning can give way to afternoon cloud and brief hail in July/August. Wind is constant at the summit; always carry a windproof outer layer.

What to See

The Summit Saddle

A broad, open saddle roughly 400m wide, with views opening simultaneously north to the Kashmir Valley and south to Kishtwar highlands.

Alpine Meadows (Margs)

Sweeping meadows painted with wildflowers in July–August, used by Gujjar and Bakerwal shepherds as seasonal pasture.

Bringi Valley Views

A deep, forested gorge visible from the drive up from Daksum, part of the Bringi Wildlife Sanctuary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact latitude and longitude of Sinthan Top? +
Approximately 33.4833°N, 75.4500°E. Search "Sinthan Top" on Google Maps and the pin falls very close to this location.
Is the road to Sinthan Top safe? +
Generally safe with a capable vehicle and experienced driver in the open season (June–October). Avoid driving after dark or immediately after heavy rain, and use a local guide on your first visit.
Can I ride a motorcycle to Sinthan Top? +
Yes, a popular route for adventure motorcyclists. A capable bike (Royal Enfield Himalayan or similar) is recommended for the Daksum–Summit stretch.
Is Sinthan Top the same as Margan Top? +
No — different passes. Margan Top (3,000m) connects Pahalgam-Anantnag with Warwan Valley; Sinthan Top is higher and connects to Kishtwar.
How long does a Sinthan Top day trip from Srinagar take? +
Typically 10–12 hours total: 3.5 hours each way plus 3–4 hours at the summit and en-route stops.
Who manages the Sinthan Top road? +
The Border Roads Organisation (BRO), via its Beacon Project, which handles the annual snow-clearing and ongoing maintenance.
